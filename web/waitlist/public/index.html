What a step can open

Not a fresh start. The exact place you stopped.

Side keeps the thread of the work, not a list of tasks. A step opens the thing itself: the tab at the line you were reading, the file at the cell you were filling.

  • Chrome tabsFolded into named groups when you stop, and opened where you were reading when you come back.In this build
  • Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PhotoshopThe file you saved last opens in its own app: Word at the end, Excel at the last filled cell, PowerPoint on the last slide.In this build
  • CalendarOnly tomorrow's first start time and how many events are left today, so One more hour can count to the meeting instead of your bedtime.In this build
  • Your one lineWhat you were curious about tomorrow comes back with the step, in your own words.In this build
  • Claude CodeThe folder your last session ran in, offered again the next time you sit down.With Pro
  • Cursor, VS CodeThe file at the line you stopped at, instead of the whole project again.With Pro

Today a step opens one of these. Pro prepares several at once: the Claude Code folder, the editor at the file and the tab you were reading, in one click.

Two things other apps won't do

It won't tell you to go to bed. It won't keep score either.

One more hour

Say you're not done and Side respects it: nothing closes, and it says one true thing — how long you would sleep if you stopped now. With the calendar on, that counts to tomorrow's first event. Then it asks exactly once, at the time you pick.

“Sure, keep going. What you did so far is saved.”

By your side

Ten minutes after a step, sidy checks whether you kept working: counts only, never what you did. After three steps it can tell you which kind of step actually gets you going, from your own numbers.

No streak to keep. No score to lose. Skipping a day costs nothing.
